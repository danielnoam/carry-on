Boeing 747

The Boeing 747 is a large, long-range wide-body airliner designed and built by Boeing Commercial Airplanes in the United States between 1968 and 2023. After the 707 entered service in October 1958, Pan Am wanted a jet two and a half times its size, to cut its seat cost by 30 percent.

Design

In 1965 Joe Sutter left the 737 programme to design the 747. It was the first aeroplane called a “Jumbo Jet”, and the first wide-body airliner. Its hump-shaped upper deck holds the flight deck above the main deck, so the nose can open as a cargo door.

The 747 was conceived while air travel was growing fast in the 1960s. Boeing expected supersonic transports to take over the long routes, so the design was laid out to become a freighter simply, and the freighter outlived every passenger version.

Four engines hang from a wing swept at 37.5 degrees, large enough that the 747 cruises at Mach 0.85 while carrying more than four hundred people. The wing flexes upward by about two metres in flight.
